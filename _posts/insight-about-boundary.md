I gained insight after figuring something out when tackling problems about the toric code model: Almost every non-trivial effect arises from the boundary.

Here is a list of examples: the boundary conditions of differential equations (vibrational modes, fluid dynamics, scattering theory, distribution of fields, etc.), reflection and refraction of waves at the boundary, interface of coexisting phases, surface physics, Fermi surface, edge states of symmetry protected topological phases, the symmetry enriched topological phases and their ground state degeneracy when the boundary condition are open... and many other phenomena. In short, symmetry makes physicists' lives easier, but all of a sudden, the boundaries ruthlessly destroy them. One of the mathematical insights is that the topology highly depends on the boundary condition of a system, and it is apparent that two states are radically different if they are in systems with different topologies. Another independent reason is that the symmetry is violated at the boundary, so those physical properties protected by the symmetry disappear there.

In my memory, the first time I realized the fearfulness of boundaries was when I learned the chemical structure of diamond. Right after I learned the chemical bond theory of carbon atoms and the reason why the diamond structure is so stable due to the symmetry, I wondered what would happen at the boundary of a diamond, where the octet rule is apparently violated. Although now I have learned a lot of condensed matter physics and got part of the solutions, I still feel weird.
