I'm Chen-Chih Wang (王晨志). I was born and raised in Kaohsiung, Taiwan (Republic of China). I'm currently a graduate student majoring in theoretical condensed matter physics, focusing on strongly correlated magnetism and its hidden non-trivial entanglement structure. I earned both my bachelor's and master's degrees at National Tsing Hua University (NTHU), a lovely university located in Hsinchu, Taiwan. I love to play the piano and erhu (for those who might not know, erhu (二胡) is a Chinese traditional string instrument that uses a bow to make sounds in a way similar to playing a violin), so you are welcome to contact me to play together or discuss classical music.

# The start of the journey

I love to share the story about why I fell in love with physics and continue studying it till now. The first book that guide me into the world of physics is [圖解量子力學 (Illustrated Quantum Mechanics) by 椎木一夫](https://www.books.com.tw/products/0010543377?srsltid=AYCX9vJzQ5ybNSYZyw7rVbm7bO07eTsOBMQs4CSPLOt3VOEEKAS9ZBCR). I read this book for the first time when I was a junior high school student. Although it sounds a bit rediculous to teach a junior high school student to understand quantum mechanics, which is notorioiusly difficult even for an undergraduate student, I was facinated by the novel phenomena in the quantum realm and how physics use elegant equations to predict and describe the behavior of everything. But this is just a beginning. My curiousity about mathematics and physics was trigered by the Schrödinger equation listed in this book, and that became the start of the whole journey of studying physics untill now. My first reaction when I saw the Schrödinger equation while reading the book was, *Wow! That looks so cool! But I have no idea what it means.* I notice that I couldn't understand a single word in the equation. And what especially made me so confused is the notation of partial derivative \\(\partial\\) because I had never seen it before. To understand the equation, I looked up so many math books and finally realized it is in a field called *calculus*. I had heared calculus before, but just its name and the fact that is notoriously difficult. But I was so eager to understand the content of quantum mechanics, so I started learning calculus myself. It took me about an year to finally master fundamental calculus, and all of a sudden, I found that I could finally read the physical equations and understand the story they want to tell us. The most important point is that, I was able to *derive* those equations by myself. It was until I learnt how to use those mathematical tools to derive physical equations that I realize math is a key to accessing the core physics principles. I especially love the process of deriving equations, as it gives not only a sense of aacomplishment but the actual feeling that I truly understand the mechanism behind various physical phenomena. This is the very reason why I'm fond of theoretical physics. 

# Research Experiences

Becoming a distinguished physicist and exploring fascinating physical phenomena have always been my dream, and this is exactly why I want to pursue higher education.

## Undergraduate

When it comes to my favorite subject, I will always say condensed matter theory, especially strongly correlated systems and frustrated magnets. I found this field fascinating when I attended Prof. [Yi-Ping Huang](https://yipinghuang.github.io)’s talk about his early work on quantum spin liquids. It was also the first time I learned about *emergent phenomena*—a type of phenomenon caused by collective interactions among a large number of individuals, resulting in an ordered pattern on a larger scale (or, to be short, More is Different). To explore more in this field, I joined Prof. Huang’s research group to do undergraduate research and began studying quantum spin liquids. 

I learned a lot of professional knowledge and how a physicist works from my advisor during my undergraduate research. My first project was studying the ground state of the Kitaev honeycomb model and trying to figure out a way to express it using a tensor network, a mathematical formulation that can not only improve numerical efficiency but also reflect the rich entanglement structure of a quantum state. From this project, I learned about frustrated magnets and tensor networks through self-study. More importantly, my advisor didn’t directly give me this research topic. Instead, we had frequent discussions and exchanged ideas until the research topic started to take shape. I believe this is a valuable experience for becoming a scholar, as we need to come up with our own questions and effectively express our ideas to others in academia. This project also earned me the undergraduate scholarship funded by the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) in Taiwan.

## Gradaute (Master)

After graduation, I became a master’s student and continued doing research in Prof. Huang’s group and started collaborating with Dr. [Sungkit Yip](https://www.phys.sinica.edu.tw/directory_en.php?directory=11&id_key=25), a distinguished research fellow at Academia Sinica, Taiwan. Prof. Huang introduced me to Dr. Yip during the 2024 summer internship at Academia Sinica. During the internship, we studied the deeper entanglement structure of the Kitaev ground state, especially how the topological order forms from the many-body entanglement perspective. I learned an important research skill from Dr. Sungkit in this project: *always start from a simple example*. We started from the two-spin model to a 1D spin chain, and gradually extended the theory to the complete 2D model. Each layer provides valuable insights into how spins get entangled and ultimately leads to the complete pattern. I also applied numerical skills acquired from undergraduate courses, such as symmetry-resolved exact diagonalization and the Lanczos method, to solve the Kitaev model, which enhanced my ability to analyse physics numerically. Later, we switched our topic to the density matrix of Kitaev spin liquids because the density matrix is a powerful tool to study entanglement structure and may shed light on the quantum information description of a condensed matter system. It turned out that our work successfully revealed the organized structure of the density matrix of Kitaev spin liquids, allowing us to fully understand their entanglement spectrum. In this program, I mastered lattice gauge theory, higher-form symmetry, and quantum information-oriented tools used in many-body spin systems. This was also an important experience to practically apply my knowledge of quantum information and condensed matter acquired in university courses lectured by Prof. [Po-Yao Chang](https://sites.google.com/view/pychang/top) to real research. We wrote up the results into a paper, and it has now been published in Physical Review B (see my publication list).

Site-wide configuration
------
The main configuration file for the site is in the base directory in [_config.yml](https://github.com/academicpages/academicpages.github.io/blob/master/_config.yml), which defines the content in the sidebars and other site-wide features. You will need to replace the default variables with ones about yourself and your site's github repository. The configuration file for the top menu is in [_data/navigation.yml](https://github.com/academicpages/academicpages.github.io/blob/master/_data/navigation.yml). For example, if you don't have a portfolio or blog posts, you can remove those items from that navigation.yml file to remove them from the header. 

Create content & metadata
